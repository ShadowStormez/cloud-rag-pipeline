## GameObjects and Components

A GameObject is the basic building block of everything in a Unity scene. A player character, a coin, a wall, a camera, and a light are all GameObjects. On its own, a GameObject does nothing and has no behavior or appearance. It is an empty container with a name, an active/inactive state, and a position in the scene. Anything you see or interact with in a Unity game exists because someone created a GameObject and then gave it things to do.

What a GameObject does is defined by the Components attached to it. Every GameObject automatically has a Transform component, which stores its position, rotation, and scale in the scene. Other components add specific abilities. A SpriteRenderer draws a 2D image, a Rigidbody2D makes the object respond to physics such as gravity, a Collider2D lets it detect hits with other objects, and an AudioSource plays sound. For example, a Flappy Bird-style bird could be one GameObject with a SpriteRenderer for its look, a Rigidbody2D for falling, and a Collider2D for detecting pipes.

Scripts follow the same pattern. When you write a C# script that inherits from MonoBehaviour and attach it to a GameObject, it becomes a component too, and it can read or change the other components on the same object. Inside a script, `transform.position` gives you the Transform's current position as a Vector3 (x, y, z), and `GetComponent<Rigidbody2D>()` fetches the physics component so you can, for instance, set its velocity. This idea of building behavior by combining small components, instead of writing one large class per object, is the core design of Unity and is the starting point for nearly every game mechanic.
